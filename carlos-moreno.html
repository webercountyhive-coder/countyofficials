Advisory board
Tap to flip

A group of people a nonprofit lists as supporters or informal advisors. It's usually a lighter role than being a director or officer — advisory board members typically don't vote on the organization's decisions or file paperwork on its behalf.

Nonprofit corporation
Tap to flip

A formally registered organization, filed with the state, that isn't set up to make money for owners. Utah's registry (the Division of Corporations) is public and shows who founded it, who runs it, and its registered address.

Campaign finance disclosure report
Tap to flip

A form every candidate for county or state office has to file, listing exactly who gave money to their campaign, how much, and what the campaign spent it on. These are public record.

Director vs. officer vs. advisory board
Tap to flip

Directors and officers (like President or Treasurer) are legally responsible for a nonprofit's decisions and finances. Advisory board members generally aren't — they lend a name and reputation, but accountability sits with the directors.

01 Who he is

Carlos A. Moreno is a Venezuelan-born political asylee who immigrated to the United States in December 2009 as an international student. He earned a law degree from Rafael Urdaneta University in Venezuela and, after arriving in Utah, associate degrees from Salt Lake Community College in Political Science/Government and Homeland Security & Emergency Management — becoming SLCC's first Latino and first immigrant student body president.

He founded New Leaders for America (also referred to as "New Leaders of America" on Path Forward Utah's site), a civic-engagement organization, around 2021–2023, and was named a "Distinguished Leader of Utah" by Utah Business Magazine in 2023. He was elected to the Salt Lake County Council, District 2 — covering West Valley City, South Jordan, Kearns, Magna, Copperton, and parts of West Jordan and Herriman — in November 2024, and took office in January 2025. He is married to Norbelys Moreno; they have three sons.

Moreno told the Deseret News in a September 2019 profile that his activism began as a Venezuelan international student, after the Venezuelan government's currency controls left thousands of students abroad — including roughly 220 in Utah — unable to access money for tuition or living expenses. He organized fellow students into an activist group, Venezuelan Students Abroad, and reached out to the United Nations and the White House. Moreno said that activism led Venezuelan officials to publicly accuse him of treason and conspiracy, and that he and his wife received political asylum with the assistance of then-Sen. Orrin Hatch and then-Rep. Chris Stewart, R-Utah. By 2019, years before Path Forward Utah's founding, Moreno was already publicly describing a goal of helping immigrant and Latino Utahns understand and engage with American government.

02 The board seat

Moreno is listed as an Advisory Board Member on the public team pages of both Path Forward Utah and Hope of Utah — two Ogden nonprofits, headquartered at the same Grant Avenue address, with overlapping leadership already documented in connection with USBE District 2 member Joseph Kerry. Kerry was Path Forward Utah's chair through 2025 before being listed as an inactive director on August 24, 2026, two days before he co-hosted an education-policy event with Rep. Burgess Owens under the Path Forward Utah and Path For Utah banners.

Utah Division of Corporations records independently confirm a third nonprofit in this cluster: Path For Utah, incorporated February 3, 2026 at the same Grant Avenue address. Its current directors are Jamie Renda, Ignacio Valdez, and Jason Coates. Valdez is separately listed as a Path Forward Utah advisory board member — making him, alongside Moreno, a second confirmed name spanning more than one organization in this network.

Not established

No financial or organizational connection between Moreno's Salt Lake County Council campaign and Path Forward Utah, Hope of Utah, or Path For Utah has been documented. His donor list contains no contributions from Renda, Kerry, Wadman, or any variant of the three nonprofits' names. The board seat and the donor network below are two separate, parallel threads — not shown to intersect.
